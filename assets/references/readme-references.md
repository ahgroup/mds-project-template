# Reference PDFs

This folder is for PDFs of papers and other reference documents that you want to
keep with the project.

## Before you add a PDF here

Reference PDFs are third-party material. They are **not** covered by this
project's license, and adding one to the repository means redistributing it to
everyone who can see the repository, including everyone who creates a new
project from this template.

Check the terms before committing a PDF:

- Open-access articles are usually fine to redistribute, but the specific
  license matters. A CC BY article can be shared freely with attribution. CC
  BY-NC or CC BY-ND variants restrict commercial use or modification, which may
  conflict with how you license your own project.
- Subscription or paywalled articles usually may not be redistributed at all,
  even by their own authors. For many journals the accepted manuscript may be
  archived while the typeset publisher PDF may not.
- Being an author on a paper does not by itself grant the right to redistribute
  it. What governs is the copyright transfer or licence-to-publish agreement.

If you are unsure, do not commit the PDF. Cite the paper in
`../dataanalysis-references.bib` instead and link to a stable public record such
as a DOI, a PubMed entry, or a PubMed Central entry. That gives readers a way to
find the paper without redistributing it.

If a PDF must stay local rather than being committed, keep it outside the
repository, or in an ignored folder, and note here where it lives.

## Example references used by this template

The example products cite three papers. They are in
`../dataanalysis-references.bib` with DOIs and PubMed IDs, and the full text of
each is publicly reachable through the links below:

- Leek & Peng (2015), *Statistics. What is the question?*
  [PubMed 25721505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25721505/)
- McKay et al. (2020), *Associations between relative viral load at diagnosis
  and influenza A symptoms and recovery.*
  [PubMed 33376754](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33376754/) ·
  [PMC7751133](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7751133/)
- McKay et al. (2020), *Virulence-mediated infectiousness and activity
  trade-offs and their impact on transmission potential of influenza patients.*
  [PubMed 32396798](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32396798/) ·
  [PMC7287351](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7287351/)

Earlier versions of this template shipped PDFs of the two McKay papers in this
folder. They were removed because redistributing publisher PDFs from a public
template is not something the template can license, and every project created
from the template inherited copies of them.
